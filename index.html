Why now — the regulatory hammer

Under DORA, you answer for every change your AI makes to production. Your application logs won’t survive the audit.

DORA, MiCA, and the SEC’s cyber-disclosure rules place the liability for autonomous systems on the regulated firm — not the model vendor. When an examiner asks what your AI agents changed, and who authorized it, self-reported application logs are not evidence. They are the account of the thing being audited, written by the thing being audited.

Our approach

Autonomy at machine speed. Accountability at human standard.

We don’t slow AI down to make it safe, and we don’t trust it to grade itself. We build the discipline around it — so speed and accountability stop being a trade-off.

I

Nothing autonomous is anonymous

Every AI action in a system we build traces to a named human who authorized it. Not in principle — in evidence. The human stays the author of intent; the machines are the instrument.

II

Evidence, not self-reporting

An AI’s account of its own work is a story, not a record. Everything that matters is verified independently of the system that produced it, and preserved so it can be re-checked years later.

III

Proven on ourselves first

Our own engineering runs under the same discipline we sell — every change to our production systems, AI-made or human-made, carries the same chain of authorization and proof.
